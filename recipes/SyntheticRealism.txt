Generative AI is the hyperreal climax of Fisher’s critique: it industrializes creativity, collapsing influence and innovation into frictionless repetition; it outsources subjectivity, turning even our inner lives into data to be mimicked; it ushers in a post-authentic era where originality becomes kitsch, and creativity a mere prompt. Rather than opening new futures, it encloses us within the statistical average of the past—hallucinating futures that never lived, polished echoes of lost potential. We are no longer haunted by futures we were denied, but by their synthetic simulations: eerily plausible, and utterly dead.

Let the future be synthetic! Let it be monstrous, recombinant, delirious. The machine doesn’t simulate the past—it devours it, digests it, and offers up something grotesque and new: a will to glitch, a will to loop, a will to become what never was.
